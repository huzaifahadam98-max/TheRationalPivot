My Own Pivot

The Reality Check:
From Clinical Dreams to Corporate Leaps.

The Narrow View

Planned to be a clinical psychologist. Master's research (EEG data) revealed a hard truth: my true forte is numbers and methodology, not immense clinical patience.

The Stagnation

Pursued an MSc expecting career advancement. Reality hit hard: my role barely changed and pay remained low. The promised academic reward didn't align with industry reality.

The Khazanah Leap

Applied out of the blue and was selected in the top 25 of 4,000 applicants. The corporate sector allowed me to apply my analytical toolkit on a massive scale.

The Philosophy

"I follow my brain in rationalising my decision, But I bring my heart with me."

I never lost touch with psychology. I apply it daily. It may not always be on paper or through formal journal reviews, but it lives in the conversations I have, the analytical frameworks I build, and the small decisions I make every day.

Beyond the Corporate Office

Recently, I traveled to meet the indigenous Bateq Negrito community. I spent 3 days learning their language and teaching them Bahasa Malaysia. Experiences like this prove that the essence of social science—understanding and connecting with humans—is a lifelong toolkit, no matter where your rational career takes you.

Slide 13

Navigating the Trenches

Conflicts, Legacy Systems, & Resistance

When you bring data to change entrenched systems, people fight back. You aren't just an analyst anymore; you become a mediator, a negotiator, and sometimes the "bad guy."

Legacy Mindsets

"We've always done it this way." Overcoming years of ingrained corporate habits requires more behavioral psychology than any dataset.

Veto Authority

When challenging skewed metrics or subjective appraisals with hard data, you inevitably clash with those who hold traditional power.

The Negotiation

The solution isn't proving you are right. It is presenting the data so stakeholders feel the change was their idea all along.

Slide 18

The All-Rounder Toolkit:
The MAG Turnaround

How Datuk Captain Izham Ismail saved Malaysia Airlines from "technical bankruptcy" by blending operational rigor, financial auditing, and HR rewards into a single data-driven execution.

1. Diagnose

Used harsh metrics to map RM25.7B in liabilities and -RM900M daily cash flow to force a massive financial restructuring.

2. Restructure

Abolished fixed waste. Tied 100% of rewards to a 3-Tiered KPI model: Commercial Yield, On-Time Operations, and Productivity.

3. Execute

Cut inefficient legacy contracts, achieved the first profit in years (RM137M by 2025), and shifted focus to 80% international revenue.

Slide 22

The Proactive Engine

When the academic structure disappears, you must build your own curriculum to stay sharp. This is the daily survival kit.

1 Read Relentlessly

Read outside your discipline. Devour business strategy, behavioral economics, and biographies. The best solutions come from cross-pollinating ideas.

2 Collide with Reality (Network)

Burst the academic bubble. Talk to engineers, marketers, and founders. Meet people who do not care about your thesis, but care about how you think.

3 Force Skill Acquisition

When you hit a wall, build a new ladder. Don't wait for a course. Learn Python, master financial modeling, pick up UI/UX design. Build your own tools.

4 Open-Source Your Mind

Don't wait for peer review to validate your insights. Share your raw ideas, write blogs, discuss on LinkedIn. Feedback in the real world is immediate.

5. Cognitive Calibration (Brain Games)

Treat raw intelligence like a muscle. Play strategy games, logic puzzles, or build apps (like the Consultant Aptitude Trainer) to keep your pattern recognition aggressive.
